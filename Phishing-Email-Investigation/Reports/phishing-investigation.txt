PHISHING EMAIL INVESTIGATION REPORT

Project: Phishing Email Investigation & Threat Intelligence Analysis
Sample: sample-1001.eml
Environment: Isolated SOC training lab
Investigation Type: Email triage, authentication analysis, IOC identification, threat intelligence

1. OBJECTIVE
Analyze a suspicious email sample as a Tier-1 SOC analyst. The investigation focuses on sender identity, email authentication results, message infrastructure, suspicious attachments, threat-intelligence reputation, and overall phishing likelihood.

2. EMAIL HEADER FINDINGS
Visible From: support@mail.coinbase.com
Recipient: noreply-KHWG3@mail.authenticatehelp.com
Message-ID: <a257a346-80ff-eb01-fe6f-e4e1b6464319@medisept.com.au>
Date: Wed, 26 Jul 2023 01:28:02 +0000
Return-Path: mssggeauthenticl-cbspprt-325937197367@medisept.com.au

Key observation:
The visible From address presents the message as Coinbase-related, while the Message-ID and Return-Path reference medisept.com.au. This infrastructure mismatch is a significant phishing indicator.

3. EMAIL AUTHENTICATION ANALYSIS
SPF: none
DKIM: none
DMARC: none
Received-SPF: None; medisept.com.au does not designate permitted sender hosts

Interpretation:
The supplied header evidence does not show successful SPF, DKIM, or DMARC authentication. Combined with the mismatch between the visible sender identity and message infrastructure, this increases suspicion.

Limitation:
Authentication results alone do not prove maliciousness. They must be evaluated with the sender identity, message content, attachments, and threat intelligence.

5. THREAT INTELLIGENCE ANALYSIS
Source: VirusTotal
Indicator: medisept.com.au
Observed result: 0 / 91 security vendors detected the domain
Community score: 0
Last analysis shown: 27 days ago

Interpretation:
At the time of analysis, VirusTotal showed no security-vendor detections for medisept.com.au. This does not establish that the email is legitimate. Domain reputation is one data point and may not reflect abuse, compromise, or a phishing context.

6. PHISHING ASSESSMENT
Overall Verdict: LIKELY PHISHING / SUSPICIOUS EMAIL

Reasons:
- The visible sender claims to represent Coinbase.
- Message-ID and Return-Path reference medisept.com.au rather than the visible Coinbase identity.
- SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are not authenticated in the supplied header evidence.
- The email contains a Word attachment named Coinbase -15392.docx.
- The sender presentation and attachment are consistent with social-engineering behavior.

Counter-indicator / limitation:
VirusTotal showed 0/91 vendor detections for medisept.com.au, so the domain itself was not independently confirmed as malicious.

7. MITRE ATT&CK MAPPING
Candidate technique: T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment
Rationale: The email contains an attachment presented in a brand-related context, consistent with attachment-based phishing delivery.
Mapping confidence: Medium. The sample is a training case and the attachment was not opened or executed.

8. SOC RESPONSE RECOMMENDATION
- Quarantine the message and prevent user interaction with the attachment.
- Search mail logs for other recipients of the same message.
- Search for the sender, Return-Path, Message-ID, domain, and attachment filename.
- Analyze the attachment only in a controlled malware-analysis environment.
- Check endpoint telemetry for evidence that the attachment was opened or executed.
- Escalate if related malicious activity is identified.

10. LESSONS LEARNED
- The visible From address should not be trusted on its own.
- SPF, DKIM, and DMARC provide authentication context but must be interpreted together.
- Message-ID and Return-Path can reveal infrastructure that differs from the displayed sender.
- A clean VirusTotal reputation does not automatically make an email trustworthy.
- Attachments should be analyzed safely without opening them on a normal endpoint.
- Phishing triage requires correlation of multiple indicators.

11. FINAL CONCLUSION
The sample was assessed as LIKELY PHISHING / SUSPICIOUS based on sender/infrastructure mismatch, lack of authenticated SPF/DKIM/DMARC results, and the presence of a suspicious brand-themed Word attachment. VirusTotal did not identify medisept.com.au as malicious at the time of checking, so the domain was not independently classified as malicious.

This investigation demonstrates:
Email received -> Header analysis -> Authentication checks -> IOC extraction -> Threat intelligence -> Risk assessment -> MITRE mapping -> Recommended response.

The investigation was performed using a safe training sample in an isolated lab.
